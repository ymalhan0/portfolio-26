INTRO

Utilities must prove every required inspection on time, but missing records and disruptions put deadlines at risk.

Regulators require utilities to inspect and maintain assets on schedule, and to prove it with complete work records: inspections, repairs, service histories, time and materials.

The problem

Manual entry, planning, and prioritization

Up to 30–45 minutes per work order across many screens, plus offline analysis to decide what to do first.

Late discovery resulting in scrambling

Teams rerun reports near deadlines where they find and hunt for missing records by hand, which is even harder when storms or short staffing disrupt plans.

If a utility can’t prove it’s up to code in time, it risks costly fines or even lawsuits.

Building on research with 4 utilities, I owned the concepts and prototypes for two connected directions: near-term workflows for creating and completing work, and an agentic command center showing where AI can take it next.

Research · Jul–Aug 2026

Before exploring solutions, we studied how compliance work actually happens.

Customers told us assembling audit documentation was painful, but we lacked a clear picture of their day-to-day workflows. To map the flows and needs, we interviewed 4 users from 4 utilities.

OUR USERS

Planner / Scheduler

Spot at-risk work early

So crews can be prioritized before an obligation is missed.

Field Technician

Capture work right the first time

So finished work counts, with no fixes later.

Compliance Manager

Prove compliance quickly

With the full evidence trail in one place, ready for audits.

What we found

  1. 01

    Creating work is slow and has too many steps.

    Need: faster, guided work creation.

    “Creating work order is cumbersome too… it’d take those guys on average 30 to 45 minutes to fill one work order.”

  2. 02

    Records are checked and corrected by hand, so gaps slip through.

    Need: missing or incorrect records flagged before submission.

    “Probably the most challenging is just checking the data and going back and making corrections. For now, it’s human putting in the data…”

  3. 03

    Budget, asset and crew data live in separate systems.

    Need: the context for a decision all in one view.

    “All of our funding lives in our ERP… So we don’t have all of those costs inside WACS… they do have to go back and reference the ERP system…”

  4. 04

    Prioritization is a judgment call, not a formula.

    Need: recommendations that explain themselves and leave the decision with people.

    “Currently we have another system to do that kind of prioritization, but the prioritization at the end is going to be manual. It’s not automated.”

Design challenge · Sep–Nov 2026

How might we make compliance work easier to capture today, and show where AI can take it next?

Sep 2026 · Designing for CAB

We started exploring solutions based on what users needed now.

Research kept pointing to pain points with the basics in the workflows: slow work creation and gaps found too late. So I designed three near-term concepts as a starting point to validate direction with users.

  1. 1

    Guided work creation

    Create work in one guided flow to reduce time on task

    • One flow, no extra navigation
    • Prefilled from where you start
    • Multi-select search in drawers

    Impact

    Before: creating work took too long, so users skipped fields, hurting data quality.

    Now: one guided, prefilled flow makes complete records easier to capture.

    Before

    After

  2. 2

    Completion view

    See done and missing records in one place.

    • Crews enter all records in one view
    • Gaps flagged for supervisors

    Impact

    Before: records were checked by hand, so gaps were caught at the last minute.

    Now: crews and supervisors see what’s missing while the work is still fresh.

  3. 3

    Compliance dashboard

    Surface compliance risks early.

    • Progress by compliance type
    • Priority actions for at-risk work

    Impact

    Before: status was spread across systems, so program risks surfaced late.

    Now: managers see risks and priority actions early, in one view.

Sep 23–24 · Customer Advisory Board 

Customers liked the direction, but asked us to fix the root cause first: better capture of work before more reporting.

I presented the concepts and facilitated sessions with 4 utility customers. The compliance dashboard landed well, but the loudest requests were about creating and closing out work:

  • One work creation flow, no matter where you start
  • More prefilled fields and multi-select for assets for quick entry
  • Focus on improving experience for field crew users

Key decision

Prioritize work creation and completion, starting with field crews; sequence the compliance dashboard after. The dashboard tested well, but it can only report on the data it’s given. With the team, we agreed to fix how records are created and captured first - aiming for a near term release.

Sep 21–Oct 28 · AI World vision

An agentic command center where agents prepare and people decide.

Leadership saw WACS as a strong fit to show agentic potential at Oracle AI World. With a tight timeline, we built on the compliance work already underway, since research had given us a real, well-understood problem. I shaped the story and flow, designed it in Oracle’s new agentic templates, and built the demo with Codex in about 3 weeks. It ran at the AI World booths (Oct 25–28) for hundreds of attendees.

The demo story follows one compliance manager through a tough week: missing records and a storm, both threatening a deadline. 

CHALLENGE · CLARITY OF PURPOSE

Screens without a story don’t show what an agent can do.

With limited time, we rushed the future script and got little feedback. Thus, the first iteration showed the new screens, but it was hard to see the agentic value. So we paused and rewrote the story, using the same research but focusing on the new agentic role.
The four steps below are that story: each one shows the agent doing work the manager used to do by hand.

  1. 1

    THE SCENARIO

    A storm and missing records put deadlines at risk.

    • 6 finished inspections blocked by missing service histories
    • 36 hydrant inspections at risk from a storm 

    Impact

    Before: gaps and disruptions surface late across separate systems.

    Now: agents flag them early in one view.

  2. 2

    Revised plan

    Agents propose a new plan to mitigate disruption.

    • Agent reasoning combines weather, asset condition and crew capacity
    • Shows rationale, benefit and cost of delay

    Impact

    Before: replanning meant manual, offline analysis.

    Now: managers review a ready plan with the reasoning shown, allowing for quicker decisions.

  3. 3

    MAKE CHANGES

    Managers view and adjust the plan through natural language.

    • “Avoid overtime” defers the 3 lowest-priority visits
    • Agent makes cost and schedule changes right away

    Impact

    Before: any change meant reworking the schedule by hand.

    Now: one sentence easily updates the plan.

  4. 4

    Approve & track

    One approval updates the schedule, notifies crews, and starts tracking.

    • Agent applies the changes and notifies affected crews
    • Real-time progress view shows if work is on track for the deadline

    Impact

    Before: rescheduling, emailing crews and chasing status by hand.

    Now: one approval does it all, and progress is tracked.

Design principle

Agents prepare, people decide. Agents gather data, suggest plans and fill in forms; every decision stays with a person.

Reflections

From “what do we ship now?” to “what moves us forward?”

This was my first deep dive into agentic design, and it ended up in front of hundreds of people at AI World. It also changed the question for WACS from what to ship next to what to ship now that moves us toward an agentic future. Customers noticed, and felt we were acting on their feedback.

The hardest part was time. We rushed clarity of purpose, and the first prototype couldn’t show its value until we went back and wrote the future story. It taught me that clarity of purpose matters even more in agentic design, where the value is the work the user no longer has to do.

Next time, I’d lock in the future script before designing screens. For now, I’m excited to keep refining the near-term designs, each one a step toward the future customers saw at CAB.
